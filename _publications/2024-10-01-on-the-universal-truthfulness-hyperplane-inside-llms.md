**Abstract**: Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit varying levels of truthfulness across different query types and settings. In this work, we introduce the Universal Truthfulness Hyperplane (UTH), a novel framework that characterizes the decision boundary between truthful and hallucinatory responses in LLM activation space. We demonstrate that LLMs maintain a consistent, generalizable hyperplane that separates correct from incorrect reasoning, even across different domains and query formats. We further show that this hyperplane can be manipulated to improve truthfulness without fine-tuning, opening new avenues for enhancing LLM reliability.

*First author work conducted at HKUST NLP Group.*
