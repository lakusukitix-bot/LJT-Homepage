**Abstract**: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding general images, but their performance on specialized tasks like chart understanding lags far behind human-level performance. In this work, we investigate the underlying reasons for this gap by systematically analyzing the performance of state-of-the-art VLMs across different types of charts (bar charts, line charts, pie charts, etc.) and different question types. We identify a critical "perception bottleneck" in current VLMs: they struggle to accurately perceive fine-grained visual elements in charts (like axis labels, data points, and color distinctions), which severely limits their ability to answer questions about charts correctly. We further propose a simple, lightweight adaptation to existing VLMs that explicitly addresses this perception bottleneck, improving chart understanding performance by 15-20% across all major chart types. This work provides new insights into the capabilities and limitations of current VLMs and points to future directions for improving their performance on complex visual reasoning tasks.
